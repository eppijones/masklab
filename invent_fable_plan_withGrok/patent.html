Automated crochet apparatus with stitch-presentation wheel for spiral single-crochet fabrication — HEKLOMAT-1

Inventor: as designated by applicant · Technical field: textile machinery / additive fibre fabrication

Field

The invention relates to an apparatus and method for mechanically producing single-crochet (fastmaske) fabric in a continuous spiral, in particular bucket-hat shells worked from a magic ring, and to a compiler that turns a stitch-level colour program into synchronized machine motion and a digital twin of the same program.

Background / Prior Art

Hand crochet inserts a hook through a prior stitch, yarn-overs, and pulls a new loop through. Locating that prior stitch is trivial for a human and historically fatal for a machine. Published attempts include:

  • Croche-Matic (Perry, García del Castillo y López, Melenbrink — Harvard GSD, ICRA 2023): a radial 9-axis machine capable of chain, single crochet, increase and decrease in the round from a magic ring. Reported stitch success is about 50%, with a practical limit of four consecutive stitches. The failure mode is locating and holding open the previous round’s loops.
  • CroMat (HSBI Bielefeld / TU Dresden): the first industrially framed crochet machine, limited to flat rows. Its breakthrough is a bank of latch needles that hold the previous row open so the hook never searches.
  • Loom-based mechanized crochet (ACM SCF 2025); USPTO 12,497,720 and 12,454,777; Technion robotic crochet (CAADRIA 2023).

None of the above combines (i) latch-based loop presentation, (ii) a continuously expanding circular work, and (iii) tapestry colour changes on a compiled stitch stream. That combination is the subject of this application.

Summary

HEKLOMAT-1 merges CroMat’s loop-holding idea with Croche-Matic’s radial spiral by introducing a stitch-presentation wheel (20): a small wheel with preferably eight latch-needle teeth that, like a film sprocket, continuously picks up the previous round’s stitch top-loops ahead of the hook, presents each loop held open at a fixed work point, and releases it behind the hook. Loop localization becomes mechanical and deterministic at any round diameter (about 10 to 144 stitches). An increase is two hook cycles on one tooth.

Around the wheel: a turntable (12) carrying an exchangeable 3D-printed hat mandrel (14); a 4-DOF hook module (30) (plunge / lift / twist); a servo yarn-over finger (40); a six-station yarn carousel (50) with at most three active strands per round (tapestry crochet over carried yarns); a single-board computer and 3D-printer motion board (60); and a camera (70) for stitch QC.

Detailed Description

Frame and C-axis

Base frame 10 is 2020 aluminium extrusion on TPU feet. Turntable 12 rides a lazy-susan bearing and is driven by a NEMA17 stepper through a GT2 belt in a groove printed into platter 13. Angular pitch of the C-axis is 2π / Nround per completed stitch, except during an increase, when the C-axis holds.

Mandrel

Mandrel 14 is a three-piece 3D print (crown, wall, brim skirt) whose lathe profile matches the hat geometry used by the pattern compiler (stitch width ≈ 5.8 mm at a 4.0 mm hook). Pieces join by bayonet so a size or style can be swapped without tools.

Presentation wheel

Wheel 20 carries eight latch-needle teeth on a 3D-printed core. A cam or servo opens the latch in pick-up zone 22, holds it open through present zone 24 (the work point, fixed in world coordinates), and closes it in release zone 26. Indexing is 2π/8 per stitch, phase-locked to the C-axis. Tooth geometry is a latch needle of knitting-machine type, 3–4 mm gauge, clipped to the printed seat.

Hook module

Carriage 30 runs on two MGN7H rails. Plunge and lift are a leadscrew or belt on a NEMA17; twist is a small servo on the collet 32 holding a 4.0 mm crochet hook. The four degrees of freedom are plunge, lift, twist, and the shared C-axis of the work.

Yarn-over finger and carousel

Finger 40 is an MG90S horn that sweeps yarn across the hook throat twice per stitch (FIG. 3c, 3e). Carousel 50 holds six mini-spools and snaps the live colour to the feed eyelet on COL. Tapestry carry: unused live strands are laid over and crocheted in, under light tension from arm 42. No more than three of six stations are live in one round.

Control, QC, compiler

Raspberry Pi 4B plus a 3D-printer board (e.g. BTT SKR Mini E3) run a 12 V motion bus. Camera 70 (Pi Camera 3) looks at zone 24; a missed latch aborts the stitch and retries. The compiler expands a run-length hat program to typed arrays {N, roundStart, colorIdx, isInc, changeAfter} and a timebase tStart with 8 s per stitch plus a 4 s colour-change penalty. The digital twin consumes the identical arrays.

Claims

  1. An apparatus for spiral single-crochet fabrication comprising: a rotary work support (12); a stitch-presentation wheel (20) having a plurality of latch teeth that pick up, present open, and release successive loops of a previous round at a fixed work point; and a hook module (30) arranged to plunge through the presented loop, yarn-over, and pull a new stitch, whereby loop localization is mechanical and independent of round diameter.
  2. The apparatus of claim 1, wherein the hook module is mounted on linear rails and provides plunge, lift and twist.
  3. The apparatus of claim 1, further comprising a servo-driven yarn-over finger (40) synchronized with the hook cycle.
  4. The apparatus of claim 1, wherein the wheel indexes by 2π/T per stitch, T being the tooth count, and is phase-locked to the rotary work support.
  5. The apparatus of claim 1, wherein the work support carries an exchangeable 3D-printed mandrel (14) whose profile matches a compiled hat geometry.
  6. The apparatus of claim 1, further comprising a camera (70) aimed at the present zone for loop-capture verification.
  7. The apparatus of claim 1, configured to fabricate a bucket hat as a continuous spiral of single crochet without decreases.
  8. A stitch-presentation wheel for a crochet apparatus, comprising a hub and a circumferential array of latch-needle teeth, each tooth movable between a pick-up attitude, a present-open attitude at a predetermined work point, and a release attitude.
  9. The wheel of claim 8, wherein the teeth number from 6 to 12, preferably 8.
  10. The wheel of claim 8, wherein each latch is opened by a stationary cam in the pick-up zone and closed in the release zone.
  11. The wheel of claim 8, wherein an increase stitch is performed as two hook cycles on a single tooth without indexing.
  12. A method of machine tapestry colour change during spiral single crochet, comprising: presenting a loop of a previous round on a latch tooth; selecting among at most three live yarns of a multi-station carousel; wrapping the selected yarn by a yarn-over finger during hook pull-through; and carrying the unselected live yarns under tension so they are enclosed by the new stitch.
  13. The method of claim 12, wherein the carousel has six stations and at most three are live in a given round.
  14. The method of claim 12, wherein colour change occurs during pull-through of the stitch that precedes a new colour.
  15. The method of claim 12, wherein a stitch cycle occupies about 6 to 10 seconds at unity speed.
  16. A system comprising a compiler that expands a stitch-level pattern (Maskekode: MR, FM, INC, COL, SLST, FO) into motion opcodes and a digital twin that animates an apparatus of claim 1 from the same opcodes, so that predicted time, colour and C-axis angle match the physical machine.
  17. The system of claim 16, wherein the compiler emits run-length colour runs and an increase index per round and expands them to typed arrays for allocation-free playback.
  18. The system of claim 16, wherein the twin reveals a fabric shell with a clipping plane at the current round height and instance-counts stitches in working order.

Abstract

An affordable automated crochet apparatus forms spiral single-crochet fabric, including tapestry bucket hats, by presenting each previous-round loop on a latch-tooth wheel at a fixed work point so a 4-DOF hook never searches. A rotary mandrel, yarn-over finger, six-station carousel and camera QC complete the machine. A compiler emits the same opcode stream to the motion controller and to a digital twin. Typical stitch time is eight seconds; a 3 694-stitch hat is about 9 h 48 m at unity speed. (148 words)
