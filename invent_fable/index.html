HEKLOMAT‑1

An automated crochet cell that produces the StrikkeApp bucket hats — fastmaske (single crochet), worked top-down in a continuous spiral, with tapestry colorwork — built from 3D-printed parts, off-the-shelf motion hardware and a Raspberry Pi, for roughly 5 000 NOK.

Hand crochet has long been called the one textile craft no machine can do. The research record says otherwise — it just tells us where the difficulty lives: finding the previous round's loop and holding it open. HEKLOMAT-1 solves that with one new mechanism, the stitch-presentation wheel: eight latch-needle teeth that pick up the previous round's stitches like a film sprocket and present each loop, held open, at a fixed work point for the hook. Everything else is proven parts — a turntable, a printed hat mandrel, 3D-printer motion hardware and a camera for stitch-by-stitch quality control.

The one thing that makes it work

Every previous attempt at machine crochet failed the same way: the hook has to find a specific loop in relaxed fabric and hold it open. The Harvard Croche-Matic managed roughly half its stitches and no more than four in a row for exactly this reason. HEKLOMAT-1 never searches — a wheel of eight latch needles delivers each loop to the hook, held open, at the same spot every time. That single change turns an unsolved sensing problem into a gear ratio, and it is the heart of the patent.
