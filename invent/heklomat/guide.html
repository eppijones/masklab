Assembly — 15 steps

1
Square the four 2020 rails into a 480 × 480 mm frame using the printed corner feet. Diagonals must match within 1 mm.
2
Bolt the lazy-susan bearing to the frame centre, then the printed hub adapter on top. Check it spins with no wobble.
3
Drop the platter onto the hub (8 × M5). Mount the C-axis motor on its printed foot and run the GT2 belt into the rim groove. Slide the motor to tension, then lock.
4
Bayonet the three mandrel sections together — brim skirt, wall, crown — and bolt the skirt spider to the hub. Twist until each section clicks.
5
Stand the column on the rear rail and bolt the cross-arm pointing at the turntable centre. Check plumb with a spirit level.
6
Screw the radial MGN7 rail to the underside of the cross-arm; slide the carriage on from the open end (keep the balls in!).
7
Bolt the hook carriage to the radial carriage and fit the two vertical plunge rails. The carriage must fall under its own weight — no binding.
8
Cut the handle off a 4.0 mm hook, slide it into the collet, and clamp with one M3 — throat facing the machine's front.
9
Press eight latch needles into the wheel core slots, hooks all facing the same way round. Lock each with its M3 grub screw, then fit the cover.
10
Mount the wheel arm to the tool body and hang the wheel at the end. Leave the slot loose — mesh depth is calibration step 2.
11
Screw the MG90S into its bracket and press the yarn-over finger onto the spline. Centre the servo before fitting the finger.
12
Assemble the carousel drum on its post, press in six spool spigots, and belt it to the third stepper.
13
Thread the yarn: spool, up over the spring tension arm, through the three eyelets, into the finger's tip notch, and leave a 30 cm tail at the hook.
14
Mount the Pi in its case and the SKR board beside it. Wire the five steppers and two servos, then the 12 V supply last.
15
Clamp the camera pod to the tool stalk and aim it at the work point. Focus manually until a single stitch fills a third of the frame.

Calibration

1 · C-axis zero and pitch

Mark one platter stud with tape. Command 100 index moves and confirm the mark returns exactly to the start. If it drifts, the belt is slipping — increase tension, don't change the step count.

2 · Wheel mesh depth (the one that matters)

Hand-crochet 12 stitches onto the mandrel to give the wheel something to bite. Loosen the arm slot and lower the wheel until each latch needle enters a top loop without touching the mandrel — typically 2.5–3.5 mm engagement, one hook-diameter. Tighten the slot, then hand-turn the platter through a full revolution: every tooth must pick up exactly one loop and drop it cleanly. This is the invention working; everything else is servo trim.

3 · Hook plunge depth and yarn-over sweep

Jog the plunge until the hook throat sits just below the presented loop, and set that as P = 1.0 (nominally 18 mm). Then jog the servo so the finger lays the yarn into the throat, not on the shank. Save both to config.json.

4 · Chain test

Run heklomat test --chain 10. Ten chain stitches with even loops means the cycle timing is right. Uneven loops → raise tension-arm preload by 5 g and repeat.

Your first hat

  1. Load the yarn colors the pattern lists into carousel stations 1–n, in palette order.
  2. Hand-start the magic ring: 10 fastmasker onto the mandrel crown, working loop left on the machine hook. (Automating the magic ring is v2 — it is 30 seconds of the ten hours.)
  3. Pick the pattern in the web UI and press Start. Watch the first full round with a finger on the stop button.
  4. Check in after round 20 — that is where colorwork density peaks (up to 26 changes per round on the NORGE kits).
  5. Expect 7–10 hours unattended. Fasten off by hand, weave in two ends, steam lightly over the mandrel, and let it dry on the mandrel to set the brim.

→ Watch the whole run in the 3D simulation first — it uses the same instruction stream and the same cycle times, so the clock it shows is the clock you'll get.
